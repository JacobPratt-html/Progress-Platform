Purpose of this document

To inform a strategic discussion on the different functions of our team, how we can work on different policy tracks, and the potential outputs from each of those tracks.

Section 01

Overview

PAI has released an updated Strategic Plan with a renewed focus on three areas of work: AI & the Value Chain; AI & Human Connection and Wellbeing; and AI & Shared Prosperity. Policy plays a key role across all three areas.

We can now redesign the policy team to improve how we design and develop guidance that changes how policymakers make decisions, and ultimately support a more just, equitable and prosperous world.

This document reviews how this could be done — looking at the core functions of our policy team regardless of P&R work, potential functions that align with and support the three focus areas, and the potential outputs across the core function and three areas. It closes with key questions for discussion.

Section 04

Output options

Our research and recommendations will be packaged as outputs. These can be PAI-authored or co-authored, depending on the level of collaboration in developing them, and can take various levels of effort to produce.

AuthorshipOutputDescriptionEffortExample
PAI Authored Statement A short statement positioning PAI on a certain topic Low —
Blog post A written article describing an issue or relevant policy development Low —
Policy brief A document providing a PAI point of view on a narrow topic Medium Stanford: Validating Claims about AI
Consultation or RFI response A response to a government consultation Medium —
Taxonomy A concise presentation of concepts relevant to a topic area Medium Glossary for Synthetic Media Transparency Methods
Framework A structured approach for organising thinking and guiding action on a topic High Guidance for Safe Foundation Model Deployment
Full report A comprehensive treatment of a topic, presenting PAI's research, analysis, and recommendations High Strengthening the AI Assurance Ecosystem
Co-Authored Joint statement A short statement co-signed with partners to express a shared position on a topic Low —
Workshop summary A write-up capturing the discussions and key takeaways from a convening of stakeholders Low —
Co-produced policy brief A point of view on a narrow topic, developed jointly with one or more partners Medium —
Co-developed taxonomy A presentation of concepts in a topic area, developed collaboratively with partners Medium Glossary for Synthetic Media Transparency Methods
Co-developed framework A structured approach to a topic, developed collaboratively with partners to guide action across organisations High Guidance for Safe Foundation Model Deployment
Multistakeholder consensus report A comprehensive report reflecting agreement reached among diverse stakeholders through a structured, collaborative process High Strengthening the AI Assurance Ecosystem

Section 05

Questions for discussion

Prioritisation & resourcing
  • How do we prioritise our work across all these areas given our limited resource? What do we consciously choose not to do?
  • Do we go deep in one or two focus areas and functions, or maintain a lighter presence across all three? What is the cost of spreading ourselves thin?
  • Which area has the clearest policy window over the next 12 months, and should that shape our sequencing?
Team & capability
  • How can we hire a team to cover all these areas effectively? Which functions need dedicated expertise, and which can be shared across the three of us?
  • What mix of skills do we need: domain specialists for the three areas, or generalist policy translators who can work across them?
Working with P&R
  • How can we complement existing policy-focused research and recommendations, such as in AI & Shared Prosperity, rather than duplicating them?
  • How do we sequence policy work against research timelines — do we wait for research to mature, or run foresight and positioning in parallel? Does this vary by theme?
Outputs & impact
  • Which outputs give us the most impact per unit of effort? Should we favour frequent, lower-effort outputs or fewer flagship pieces?
  • How many co-authored / consensus outputs can we realistically sustain, given their high coordination cost?
